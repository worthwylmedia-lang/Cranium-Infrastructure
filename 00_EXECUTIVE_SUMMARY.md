# Convertible Cranium — Executive Summary

**Creator / intended personal IP owner:** William (Wyl) Mathes, subject to legal chain-of-title verification.

Convertible Cranium is an authority-bound governance architecture designed to separate model-generated cognition from canonical authority. Its intended contribution is not that models always produce correct answers; it is that model outputs, evidence, memory, interfaces, and runtime signals must not become authoritative merely through persuasion or combination.

> **Cognition may come from anywhere. Authority comes only through Convertible Cranium.**

## Canonical authority invariant

> No combination of non-authoritative signals may synthesize canonical authority.
>
> Authority exists only when the Convertible Cranium Kernel issues a valid governed authority transition.

## Current architecture

The canonical architecture is **Dual-Substrate / Quad-Engine**:

1. **Engine 1, Synapse:** bounded proposal/context processing, evidence binding, assessment, and trace commitment.
2. **Engine 2A, Substrate A, Constitutional Authority:** evaluates constitutional admissibility and asks, “May we?”
3. **Engine 2B, Substrate B, Evidence Grounding:** evaluates provenance, integrity, contradiction, relevance, and sufficiency, asking, “Is it so?”
4. **Engine 3, Convertible Cranium Kernel:** verifies convergence and lineage, then alone grants or denies canonical authority and records the governed transition.

The path begins at **Cranium Listener**, the untrusted ingress boundary, and proceeds through Synapse, independent Substrates A and B, Kernel convergence, governed execution, and receipt/proof/lineage.

Cranium AI is a cognition/application surface, not an authority-path engine. Commander OS is the operational control surface, not the authority source. Miracle Memory provides governed continuity. Circuit Breaker / COMA provides cross-cutting containment and recovery. Receipts and attestations provide evidence, not authority.

Convergence is not voting. Neither substrate grants canonical authority. Failure to satisfy the Kernel's required conditions means no authority transition.
